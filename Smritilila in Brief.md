# Smṛtilīlā Essentials

**A world is what is remembered.** Smṛtilīlā is a world as memory. A language model tells each moment, and everyone present keeps their own account of it: what they saw, what was said, what it meant to them. The world is the sum of memories, and each new moment is told from them and the actions players choose.

**The engine knows no qualities.** Beneath the telling is an engine that knows very little. It knows what is in what, who was there, and when. It does not know what a sky is, or a road, a coin, a curse. Those are words the world carries and the teller reads, and the engine keeps them without reading them. So any world can be told: a frontier town, a haunted city, a moon with no sky at all.

**There is no ground truth.** Because a world is its memories, there is no hidden record of what really happened. Two people remember one evening differently, and neither is wrong. What someone is lives in what others remember of them. Agreement is something a world arrives at, the way people do.

**Things are in things.** Smṛtilīlā has no maps except the map someone remembers. A cup is in a coat, the coat is on a man, the man is in a room, the room is in a house. A place where everyone inside shares what happens is a scene. Places beside each other are neighbours, and a door or a window or a portal or a telephone can join two. That is all the space there is. Distance and direction live in the telling, and no one misses the map.

**People carry their own time.** Each person has their own present. Two players can be hours apart and never trouble each other until their paths meet. The world's own people follow the players and never run ahead of them, and no one is carried forward in time without agreeing to it: a long journey asks first.

**The world lives on its own.** The people, the creatures, the places, do not wait for you. Each in turn acts from their own memories. Quiet places rest until something touches them. News of what matters often travels slowly, from mouth to mouth, the way talk does. Sometimes it travels fast.

**Choices leave scars.** Nothing in Smṛtilīlā is ever rewritten. What was done stays done, and what was remembered stays remembered, even when it proves mistaken. What changes is what people make of it afterwards. That is why choices here matter: they last and change the world.

**Players are free, and unprotected.** A player's words and thoughts are their own. No one else's turn will speak for them or decide what they believe. But the body they play belongs to the world, and the world can hurt it, or appear to end it.

**Each world sees in its own way.** What the dead are, whether minds are private, whether a house can wake, how luck falls: each world says so in words set on its places, and the nearest place speaks loudest. A marsh can keep its own dead while the town beside it buries theirs, and the same engine lives under both.

# For Players

This is a shared, living world. You are one person or creature in it, and the world goes on around you whether or not you are watching.

**Choose a character** and describe who you are in your own words: what you do, what brings you, who you know, what matters to you. Everything you state about yourself is remembered forever and shapes your world.

Then write what your character does or says, as you would tell it. A narrator answers with what happens. You may attempt to do anything your character could do here. You cannot decide what others do, only what you do.

**Act** sends what you wrote. Alone, that is all: the narrator answers with what happens in the world. Other players may be here at the same time. When several are, the world moves in rounds: everyone writes their action and presses Act, and the story goes on when all have. You can see who is in the round and who has acted, never what they wrote. Your action stays in the box; if you type again it is taken back until you press Act again.

**Go** appears when others are in the round. It closes the round without waiting for everyone: once those who have not acted have stopped typing. Anyone who had not acted does their first suggested action. You can press Go only after you have acted.

**Who else is here.** When another player or llm controlled bot is online, a line above the box lists them: players online: you · Mira · Tomas. A name in yellow, like Mira, is level with you, in your place at your minute, so you act together. A name in light violet, like Tomas, is elsewhere, in place or in time; hover over it to see where and when. Click a violet name and the page asks, for example, "Join Tomas: advance to 3:40 pm and go to the ferry landing?". Answer Yes and your time advances and you move.

**Offline** defaults to frozen, when frozen the world cannot affect you. Change it to waiting and your character will respond to the world based on your character's memories.

**Time.** Each character carries their own present, and an action takes as long as it would, so characters who are apart run at their own pace -- one can ride a day while another waits an hour. If an action would carry you more than half an hour ahead (each world sets its own limit), the page asks first: "You are about to jump ahead ... Proceed? Y / N". N does nothing, and neither does typing a new action instead.

**Luck.** Each turn a random luck is drawn which affects the outcome of whatever you attempt. It can change the outcome of your actions from providential to ruinous though most of the time it will be in the middle. Ruinous when talking with a good friend might result in mild irritation, ruinous while scaling a cliff is a different matter entirely.

**The retire button** permanently releases your character to live in the world and they will act on their own.

# Worlds and stories

I've created numerous very different worlds with Smṛtilīlā and they all take minutes to create and work remarkably well:

- Arkham, cosmic horror lovecraft
- Greyhawk (AD&D)
- Vadhū crossing, zen social
- Early Rome. Augustus era
- American west, 1880
- Braided Isles. every isles has different time and physical laws
- Austin, Texas 2026, cellphones and AI
- Breakthrough. A DMT journey

Two stories from the first two worlds are included as examples of play.
